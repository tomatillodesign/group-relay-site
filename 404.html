Group Relay

Email one address, and everyone in the group gets a copy.

Group Relay is run by Tomatillo Design, a WordPress studio in Durham, North Carolina, for the organizations it works with. No ads, no tracking, no selling of addresses.

What you can count on

  • Every message has a leave link that works in one click
  • Only people on a group's list can write to it
  • Nobody else in the group sees your address

Why did I get an email from group-relay.org?

Someone at your church, association or neighborhood group added you to their email list, and a member of that group wrote to it. Group Relay passed the message along to you and everyone else on the list.

The sender's name is in the From line, next to the group's name, and replying works the way the group has set it up: either to the person who wrote, or to the whole group. The footer of every message says which.

How to leave a group

Either of these works right away, and you don't need an account:

  • Click Leave this group at the bottom of any message, then confirm.
  • Use the Unsubscribe button your email program shows near the top of the message.

You can undo it for 24 hours. After that, ask the group's administrator if you'd like to rejoin.

Something wrong with a message?

If you got mail from a group you never joined, or a message looks like spam or impersonation, email abuse@group-relay.org. Include the subject line and the date, and I'll look into it.

Forged senders are blocked before delivery, and addresses that bounce or mark a message as spam are removed from every group automatically.

For organizations

Group Relay replaces the old-fashioned listserv: a choir list, a committee, a block's neighbors. An administrator adds members, picks whether replies go to the sender or to everyone, and that's it. Members get a welcome note with a leave link, every post is archived, and bounces are handled for you.

Groups are set up by invitation for the organizations Tomatillo Design works with. If that's you, write to hello@group-relay.org.
